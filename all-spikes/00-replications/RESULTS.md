## R0 — environment probe

**Did the substrate work here?** Yes, with three caveats worth recording.

- **124 of 129** dysts systems produce clean, bounded, aligned trajectories on this
  machine. Five were dropped: three hit the integration deadline (`HyperLu`,
  `InteriorSquirmer`, `MacArthur`), one is degenerate, one (`SwingingAtwood`) is missing
  the `multiscale_entropy` annotation in dysts itself.
- **dysts' own integrator was unusable.** Its default is Radau at `rtol=atol=1e-12`, which
  costs **41 s for a 2,000-point Lorenz trajectory** here. We take the equations and the
  published invariants from dysts but integrate with RK45 at `rtol=1e-9`, which gives
  **20,000 points in 7 s** — about 57× faster per point, and still orders of magnitude
  tighter than the surrogate being fitted.
- **`solve_ivp` cannot be interrupted**, so one stiff system stalled the first scan
  indefinitely. Fixed with a wall-clock deadline checked inside the RHS. This is the kind
  of thing a plan cannot anticipate and a probe finds in ten minutes.

### Timing probe: the plan's cost model was wrong

Measured, 200 epochs, batch 256:

| params | 500 samples | 8,000 | 32,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,011 | 3.8 s | 11.1 s | 51.2 s |
| 9,987 | 1.0 s | 13.5 s | 55.2 s |
| 99,843 | 1.9 s | 28.3 s | **156.7 s** |

The cost model in PLAN.md predicted 3.7 s for the 1,011 × 32,000 cell. It came in at
51.2 s — **14× over**. The error was the assumed per-batch overhead: 150 µs assumed,
**~2.0 ms measured**. At these model sizes the work is dispatch-bound, not FLOP-bound, so
cost tracks the *number of optimizer steps* far more than the number of parameters. Note
the 1,011-parameter and 9,987-parameter rows are nearly identical: a 10× capacity increase
is free until the model is large enough to matter.

Two consequences followed, both acted on:

1. **Threading buys nothing.** Same three fits: 63.4 s with 2 torch threads, 62.4 s with
   1. Two single-threaded worker processes instead give ~1.7× (6 fits in 72 s versus 3 in
   62 s). All training phases now run as two shards.
2. **Larger batches were rejected.** Batch 1024 cut a fit from 30.9 s to 7.3 s but moved
   final training loss from 2.6e-6 to 1.1e-5. A worse optimiser inflates precisely the
   error floor R4 exists to measure, which would manufacture the "intrinsic ceiling"
   conclusion out of our own optimisation shortcut. Speed was taken elsewhere instead.

## R2 — how many independent axes does "complexity" have?

**Question.** The project assumes learnability and chaoticity are separate measurable axes.
That presupposes the statistics used to describe systems are not all the same number in
different clothes. If they collapse to one or two dimensions, any predictor built on them
has almost no signal to work with and the ceiling on the whole meta-model idea is low.

**Method.** 45 systems, 11 statistics, no model training. Published invariants from dysts
(largest Lyapunov exponent, Kaplan–Yorke dimension, correlation dimension, multiscale
entropy, state dimension) plus statistics computed from the trajectory (permutation
entropy, weighted permutation entropy, 0-1 chaos test, spectral entropy, our own
correlation dimension, autocorrelation time).

### Answer: H1. The space is genuinely multi-dimensional.

| | |
|---|---|
| median \|Spearman\| off-diagonal | **0.230** |
| principal components for 90% of variance | **7 of 11** |
| participation ratio (effective dimensions) | **5.62** |

Explained variance per component: 0.31, 0.20, 0.14, 0.10, 0.08, 0.07, 0.04, …

So "system properties" is a roughly 5–6 dimensional space, not a disguised scalar. **The
pre-registered failure mode for R2 did not occur**, and the residual-after-Lyapunov framing
has room to be meaningful.

### Structure worth naming

Spearman correlations, 45 systems:

Three findings:

1. **The largest Lyapunov exponent is nearly orthogonal to everything else.** Its strongest
   correlation with any other statistic is 0.43. Against attractor dimension it is 0.30
   (Kaplan–Yorke) and 0.17 (our correlation dimension). This is Gilpin's decorrelation
   result visible directly in the feature space, before any model is trained: chaoticity
   and the information-theoretic / geometric descriptions of a system are not measuring the
   same thing.

2. **Three tight clusters, and they are the expected ones.** Permutation entropy and its
   weighted variant at 0.97 (same construction). The 0-1 chaos test and spectral entropy at
   **0.89** — worth noting, since the 0-1 test is usually presented as a dynamical test and
   this says that on these systems it is largely carrying spectral information. And the
   dimension family (Kaplan–Yorke, published correlation dimension, our own) at 0.56–0.71.
   Our independently computed correlation dimension agreeing with dysts' published value at
   0.71 is also a check on our estimator.

3. **Our own correlation dimension is *negatively* correlated with spectral entropy
   (−0.08) and near-zero with the 0-1 test (0.08).** Geometric complexity and spectral
   complexity are separate axes here.

### The sampling artefact — an unplanned result

While reading R2's first output, systems with λ = 1.68 were scoring **K = −0.01** on the
0-1 chaos test: "not chaotic" for something unmistakably chaotic. Cause: trajectories are
aligned at 100 points per dominant Fourier period, following Gilpin (2023). That is heavily
**oversampled** for ordinal statistics, so at delay 1 they measure the sampling rate rather
than the dynamics.

On identical Lorenz data:

| delay | perm. entropy | weighted PE | 0-1 test K |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.248 | 0.163 | **−0.00** |
| 2 | 0.325 | 0.228 | −0.01 |
| 5 | 0.500 | 0.435 | **+1.00** |
| 10 | 0.679 | 0.616 | +1.00 |
| 20 | 0.865 | 0.821 | +1.00 |
| 40 | 0.905 | 0.889 | +1.00 |

Autocorrelation time: 21. Toker et al. (2020) include an explicit oversampling-correction
step in the Chaos Decision Tree Algorithm; we had skipped it. Fixed by setting the ordinal
delay to each channel's autocorrelation time, and **both versions are now recorded**,
because the gap is the result:

| statistic | mean naive | mean corrected | Spearman naive vs corrected |
|---|---|---|---|
| weighted permutation entropy | 0.170 | 0.641 | **0.14** |
| 0-1 chaos test K | 0.097 | 0.774 | **0.24** |
| permutation entropy | 0.220 | 0.659 | 0.59 |

**The naive 0-1 test classifies 5 of 45 systems as chaotic. The corrected one classifies
35 of 45.**

Two things follow, and the second is the more interesting.

- The correction does not merely shift values, it **scrambles the ranking**: naive and
  corrected weighted permutation entropy correlate at 0.14 across systems. They are
  effectively different variables. Any study that regressed forecast skill on delay-1
  ordinal statistics computed on timescale-aligned trajectories was regressing on
  something close to noise.
- **Timescale alignment does not equalise effective sampling.** The autocorrelation time
  ranges from **1 to 229** across the 45 systems (median 17); only 4 systems are not
  oversampled at all. So the protocol that makes systems comparable in *Lyapunov time* —
  the protocol the whole chaos-forecasting benchmark rests on — leaves them wildly
  incomparable in *information* terms. That is a criticism of the standard alignment
  convention, and it emerged from a debugging session rather than from the plan.

**Carry forward.** Every statistic used as a predictor in R3 onwards uses the corrected
version, and the naive version is retained as a control. Anything we eventually publish
must state the delay convention explicitly.

## R4 — does surrogate skill saturate?

*Running.* Note from an aborted earlier run: at n_train = 500, valid prediction time held
at 17–20 steps across capacities from 1,011 to 46,212 parameters — capacity saturation at
fixed data, which is the shape this experiment is looking for.

## R5 — is the non-monotone capacity requirement real?

*Running.* Note from the smoke test (2 knob values, reduced budget, not conclusive): the
standard map at K = 0.4 reached 1% error with ~400 parameters, while at K = 5.0 even 3,235
parameters only reached 3.5% — more chaos needing *more* capacity, the opposite direction
from NNPT. Median seed coefficient of variation of the error was 4.9%, already enough to
move a threshold-crossing capacity by a full architecture step.
